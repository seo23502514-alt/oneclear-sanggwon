La Spaziale S9 EK Compact 2 Group Espresso Machine
The La Spaziale S9 EK Compact is a two-group commercial espresso machine for cafés, restaurants, bakeries and mobile operations that need programmable volumetric dosing in a space-conscious body. Its 5-liter heat-exchange boiler, dual pressure gauge and choice of 110V or 220V configurations make it adaptable to sites where counter width or available electrical service is constrained.
The current manufacturer's U.S. specifications list a footprint of approximately 22.8 inches wide by 20.8 inches deep—dimensions that correct older listings commonly repeated online. The S9 Compact is narrower than a standard two-group machine but should still be planned as a fixed, direct-plumb commercial installation.
Why Choose the S9 EK Compact?
Compact commercial two-group layout
Two groups allow simultaneous extractions while the narrower body saves counter space. It works well where a one-group machine could become a workflow bottleneck but a full-width two-group will not fit.
Electronic volumetric dosing
Program shot-water portions for consistent operation across multiple employees. Volumetrics free the barista to steam or assemble drinks, but recipes should still be verified by dose, beverage weight and time.
5-liter heat-exchange boiler
The shared commercial boiler supports brewing and steaming at the same time. It provides useful capacity for the footprint, though milk-heavy rushes may justify a full-size machine with a 10-liter boiler.
110V or 220V configurations
The machine family is available in both electrical formats. A lower-voltage option can simplify some installations, but the exact amperage and heating wattage determine recovery. Have an electrician verify the circuit before purchase.
Boiler and pump-pressure visibility
A double gauge helps the operator or technician monitor service-boiler pressure and pump pressure. It is a diagnostic aid, not a replacement for scheduled maintenance or recipe measurement.

Buyer Fit and Capacity
The S9 Compact is appropriate when two-group access and installation flexibility matter more than maximum steam reserve. Use expected milk volume during the busiest 15 minutes to decide whether its 5-liter boiler is sufficient. Large take-away drinks consume substantially more steam than espresso or traditional cappuccino service.
Plan the whole workstation: primary and decaf grinders, knock box, pitcher rinser, tamping surface, milk refrigeration and access to service panels. A compact machine can still perform poorly in a cramped or poorly arranged bar.
Installation, Water and Maintenance
Confirm the selected model's nameplate, amperage and plug before electrical work. Provide an accessible shutoff, correctly selected treatment and compliant drain. Test hardness, alkalinity, chloride and TDS; start with our
espresso-machine water guide
.
Backflush both groups, clean baskets and screens, purge steam wands and monitor treatment capacity. Establish preventive service intervals around actual drink volume and local water conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is the S9 EK Compact volumetric?
Yes. It provides programmable electronic dose buttons plus manual delivery.
How wide is it?
Current manufacturer U.S. specifications list approximately 22.8 inches wide. Always verify the current technical sheet before building cabinetry.
Can it run on 110V?
A 110V configuration is available, along with 220V. Confirm amperage, plug and recovery requirements for the selected version.
Does 110V provide the same recovery as 220V?
Available heating wattage differs by configuration. Lower-power service may recover more slowly during milk-heavy demand.
Does it need plumbing?
Yes. It requires direct treated water and a drain.
Is this a tall-cup machine?
This listing is for the compact standard-height platform. Confirm cup clearance against the actual cups used on the menu.
Does it have PID-controlled brew boilers?
No. It is a commercial heat-exchange machine, not an independent multi-boiler design.
What size accessories fit?
Use La Spaziale-compatible 53 mm portafilters, baskets and tampers.

It is a candidate for cafés, mobile coffee businesses and commercial operators comparing heating design, temperature control, pump configuration and drink volume. La Spaziale S9 EK 2 Group Compact FAQs
Who is the La Spaziale S9 EK 2 Group Compact best suited for?
It is best suited for cafés, mobile coffee businesses and commercial operators whose priorities match the specifications and workflow described above.
What should I verify before installing the La Spaziale S9 EK 2 Group Compact?
Confirm the published voltage, circuit, water, drain and clearance requirements. Commercial installations should be reviewed with the installer before shipment.
What should I compare before choosing this espresso machine?
Compare warm-up routine, brew and steam capacity, temperature controls, water connection, maintenance access and back-to-back drink volume.
What maintenance should be planned?
Follow the manufacturer’s cleaning schedule, use suitable water where applicable and replace normal wear components when performance changes.

Moshi C83 Conical Burr Espresso Grinder
Anonymous
Great grinder, better than the Niche for less $
I enjoy experimenting with grinders and own many ranging from old commercial flat 64mm that I've had for years up to and including the Kafatek MC-6  and the Weber EG-1 Mk3. Comparing the Moshi to my Niche and the Kafatek, the massive 83mm conicals outperform the Niche, and are close to on par with the MC-6.  It works fantastic for espresso by just tossing all the beans in the hopper, but also does an amazing job with pourover by utilzing a slow feeder (I use the Aro with the Cafovo adapter).  The grinder is fun to operate, the ionizer works well, and although the description says it shuts off after 30 seconds, it actually shuts off after a short delay when it no longer has a load on the motor meaning that it stays running while utilizing the slow feeder. The dialing range is huge for both espresso and filter and the adjustment knob is smooth and precise.  One rotation of the dial equates to 200 microns of vertical movent, but due to the conical burr geometry  that equates to 5 microns of burr gap change per small tick in the espresso range.  The burrs are easy to swap, and although the Weber Key (Mazzer 10 core) burrs fit and are easy to swap in (the acorn nut is LH thread!) I found no benefit over the stock burrs (12 core).  Retention is near zero as long as the bellows are used, similar to both the Niche and MC-6. I ground the lightest roast coffee I had on hand to almost turkish fine (which stalls the EG-1), and the Moshi had no trouble. One downside compared to other more expensive grinders is it's fixed RPM which I measured at 300 under no load. It's advertised at 280 which seems accurate while grinding but I was unable to measure.  In any case, this seems to be an appropriate speed for these burrs and I doubt lower RPM would have much effect on taste  that the slow feeder wouldn't overcome. The overall build quality is great, but only time will tell if it holds up with extended use.   It does have a 1 year warranty through Espresso Outlet which means they have some confidence it should last.  Definitely recommend, probably one of the best if not the best grinder at this price point.

Turin Creyke GF64 64mm Flat Burr Coffee Grinder
G Froelich
The very low price for a 64mm flat burr grinder really sells this for me
The very low price for a 64mm flat burr grinder really sells this for me. I will be replacing the stock burrs with another set better suited for filter brews, but even then I've saved $100s. However the grind adjustment collar and associated parts need some serious attention. The collar is smooth and round, making it very difficult to turn with confidence. All other similar grinders I own or have used make it easy to grip (and lock) the collar. Also, the ground coffee can spray out and miss the metal cup, despite the antistatic element in the exit tube. I still need to mist the beans a bit to keep that to a minimum.
